04 · PREDICTION ENGINE Spatio-Temporal
Graph Neural Network

Venue nodes are gates, corridors and exits. Edges are paths. The model learns how space and time change congestion.

CORRIDOR C · 80% CONGESTION RISK
Now let me explain how the technology actually works. We designed the system around one idea: every participating phone can become both a sensor and a communication node. The phone collects anonymous movement signals such as approximate location when available, direction, speed, nearby device density and changes in crowd flow. We do not identify people; we understand how the crowd is moving. Those signals are processed locally into an aggregated crowd state. Nearby phones communicate using Bluetooth Low Energy and Wi-Fi Direct, exchanging small encrypted crowd-state messages. This creates a local mesh, so information can travel phone to phone even when cellular internet is unavailable. We represent the venue as a graph and use a Spatio-Temporal Graph Neural Network: it learns both spatial relationships and how density changes over time. It can predict that Corridor C has an 80% probability of becoming congested within minutes.

OFFLINE MODEDevices + meshLocal prediction and routing continue when internet connectivity disappears.
That prediction is passed to our routing engine. It does not simply find the shortest path; it considers predicted congestion, current density, path capacity and safety. If one route is becoming overloaded, it distributes people across alternatives before the bottleneck forms. Then we close the loop: people move, the system observes the new state, the prediction changes, routing changes, and the crowd moves again. Sense, Communicate, Predict, Redirect, Observe, Repeat. The cloud is useful for analytics, model training, monitoring and the organizer dashboard, but it is not required for core safety. In connected mode we have devices, mesh, cloud and global prediction. In offline mode, devices, mesh, local prediction and local routing continue. We are not another dashboard explaining congestion after it happens. We sense, communicate through, predict and redirect the crowd before congestion becomes critical.
